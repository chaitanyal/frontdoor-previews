## Draft

Central Texas Mental Health protects the privacy of your health information. This page summarizes your choices and rights. Read our full Notice of Privacy Practices for details about how information is used and shared, your rights, and our responsibilities.

### Access and correct your records

You can request an electronic or paper copy of your health records and ask us to correct information you believe is inaccurate or incomplete. Contact the office to make a request. If a correction request is denied, we will explain why in writing.

You may also request a record of certain disclosures of your health information and a paper copy of our privacy notice.

### Choose how we contact you

You can ask us to contact you at a particular phone number or mailing address. We will accommodate reasonable requests for confidential communication. Let the office know which contact details are safe to use.

### How your information may be used and shared

We may use and share health information for your treatment, payment for services and practice operations. Other disclosures may be permitted or required by law, including certain public-health, safety and legal purposes. The full notice explains these uses and their limits.

You can ask us to limit certain uses or disclosures. Some requests may be declined. If you pay for a service in full out of pocket, you can ask us not to share information about that service with your health insurer for payment or health care operations, unless disclosure is required by law.

### Your choices about sharing

Tell us your preferences about sharing information with family members or others involved in your care. The authorization form lets you identify recipients and the information you want released. You may revoke an authorization by notifying the office in writing; this does not undo disclosures already made in reliance on it.

Written permission is required for marketing uses or the sale of your health information. The full notice describes when information may be shared without your authorization.

### Questions or privacy concerns

Contact the office at (512) 964-6992 if you have questions, want to exercise your privacy rights or wish to raise a concern. You may also file a complaint with the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services Office for Civil Rights. We will not retaliate against you for filing a complaint.
